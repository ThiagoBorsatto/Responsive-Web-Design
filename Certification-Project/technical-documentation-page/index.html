Satisfactory

Satisfactory is often described as "First-Person Factorio," but that sells it short.

You play as an employee of FICSIT Inc., a massive interstellar corporation. You are dropped onto a lush, vibrant alien planet (Massages-2(A-B)b) with a single mission: exploit its natural resources to build massive machines for the "Save Day" initiative.

Story

The story is intentionally lean, delivered through dry, corporate-style humor.

We are the PIONEER!!!

  1. The Premise: You are a "Pioneer." You aren't here to save the planet; you’re here to strip-mine it.
  2. The Narrative: Most of the "plot" comes via ADA, the AI overseer who provides instructions with a heavy dose of passive-aggressive corporate jargon.
  3. The Mystery: As you explore, you find strange artifacts (like Mercer Spheres and Sommersloops) that hint at a deeper, slightly more sinister narrative involving the planet's history and FICSIT’s true intentions.
Gameplay

This is where the game shines. It’s a loop of Exploration → Extraction → Automation.

Find - Materials!

Extract - Materials!

Automation the find and extraction part!

Use the MAM! Use find Hard Drive!
  1. Building: You start by hand-mining iron; by the end, you have miles of conveyor belts, massive refineries, and nuclear power plants. The 3D verticality is the game-changer—you can build sprawling "mega-factories" that reach into the clouds.
  2. Logistics: The challenge lies in the math. You’ll spend hours balancing input/output rates. If your Miner produces 60 Iron Ore per minute, but your Smelter only takes 30, you’ve got a bottleneck to solve.
  3. Combat & Exploration: The world is handcrafted (not procedurally generated), meaning every cliff and cave is placed with intent. You’ll need to fight off alien "hogs" and "spitters" while hunting for rare resources like Uranium or Quartz.
Characters

There are technically very few "characters," which emphasizes the feeling of being a lone cog in a giant machine:

  1. The Pioneer: That’s you. Clad in an orange suit, you are remarkably agile but totally silent.
  2. ADA: Your only constant companion. Her "encouragement" is usually just a reminder that your efficiency is slightly below corporate standards.
  3. The Wildlife: From the adorable (and bouncy) Bean to the terrifying "Stinger" spiders, the planet feels alive—and often annoyed that you're there.
Final Opinion

Verdict: Satisfactory is a masterpiece of the "just five more minutes" genre. It manages to make complex mathematics and logistics feel like a relaxing playground. It’s meditative yet mentally taxing—a rare combination that works because of its incredible sense of scale.
